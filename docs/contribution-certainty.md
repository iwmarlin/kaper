# Certainty on contributions

`certainty` says how sure the archive is that the person made the contribution.
It does not grade the medium the evidence came in, and it is not a place to
record that a source is thin. That belongs to the source, which carries its own
`reliability`, and to the wording of the citation.

## confirmed

A source states the contribution. The label prints the orchestra, the sheet
music names the lyricist, the reissue credits the conductor, the uploader of a
transfer names the band. All of these attest the credit, and the archive records
it as confirmed while the source's `reliability` and the citation say how the
statement reached us.

A collector's upload that names a performer is a statement about the disc, made
by someone holding it. Recorded as confirmed, with the source at medium
reliability and a `scopeNote` saying where the name comes from and whether the
label was shown.

## probable

No source states the contribution and the archive infers it, or sources
disagree.

Inference: Willi Domgraf-Fassbaender took the lead in a singer's film whose
songs were published in a Schlagerheft, so he presumably sang them. Nothing said
he did, and the credit stayed probable until a published reissue named him.

Conflict: the Novaphon label of *Du bist verrückt, mein Schatz* reads "Musik und
Text von Fritz Rotter und Dr. B. Kaper", while the sheet music names Kaper for
the music alone. Kaper's credit as lyricist is probable, and the note sets the
two readings side by side rather than choosing.

Also probable: contributions of a kind the sources describe loosely — background
or cue-level music, reused pre-existing music — where the fact of the
contribution is documented but its extent is not.

## What a scopeNote is for

To say what the evidence is, in one sentence, so that a reader can weigh the
credit without opening the source: what was printed, what was only asserted,
what was not shown.
