# The archive against Sieghardt's work catalogue, 8 November 2026

Alexander Sieghardt's catalogue of Walter Jurmann's works, in Elisabeth
Buxbaum's *„Veronika, der Lenz ist da“* (2006; `SRC0373`), was compared
with the archive: the opus list, op. 001–280 (pp. 178–211), and the
filmography from its first entry to no. 62 (pp. 212–231). Every entry that
names Kaper was compared with the archive's Work, credits, titles and
dates. From 1941 on, the opus list and the filmography fall outside the
archive's years, 1902–1939. Two entries there name Kaper: *Harbour Café*
(op. 188, p. 203; Criterion Music Corporation, 1941), words by Jurmann and
Kaper, and *Blush and Sigh* (op. 194, p. 204; manuscript, 1941), words by
Gus Kahn, written for *When Ladies Meet* and not used. Neither is in the
United States copyright catalogues of 1941–1942, which register from that
film only cues by Kaper alone; they are not added.

A work list arranged by year, pp. 200–217 of Eberhard Görner's *Walter
Jurmann: Sein Leben, seine Musik* (Henschel, 2014; `SRC0992`), was used
alongside and at first taken for part of Sieghardt's catalogue. It is a
separate list, and by its own note it follows the Walter Jurmann website,
so it does not confirm the online works catalogue (`SRC0306`)
independently. Where this note first gave Sieghardt's “year-by-year list”,
it now names Görner.

Sieghardt is a secondary source, and not always consistent with himself:
his opus list titles the French *Blue Venetian Waters* *Sous le ciel de
Venise* in one entry and *Sous le ciel de Venice* in the next. Görner
differs from him in spelling, *Hi-do-ho* for his *Hi-di-ho* and *Le bistrot
du port* for his *Le bistro du port*. Where the archive holds a primary
document that disagrees with either, the document stands, and that is noted
below.

## Done

- **Sieghardt was cited for what he does not say.** *Chanson d'amour de
  Tahiti* credited Ch. Davson on Sieghardt; he names Charles Secchia and
  Jean Vella (op. 117/F). Davson now rests on the France-Mélodie edition
  alone, and the song's note gives Sieghardt's attribution. *Pourquoi donc
  verser des larmes* credited André Mauprey on Sieghardt, whose opus list
  says “unknown”; his filmography, though, credits the French film's lyrics,
  this song among them, to Mauprey (p. 221). The credit was kept as
  `probable`; it is now confirmed by the Eschig voice-and-piano edition of
  1932 in the BnF (`SRC0981`), which prints “Paroles françaises de André
  Mauprey”.
- **Authors Sieghardt adds**, credited `probable` on his catalogue alone:
  Ernst Marischka on *Ninon, lach mir einmal zu* (op. 088); Henri-Georges
  Clouzot and Serge Veber on *O Madona!* (op. 089/F); Louis Poterat on *C'est
  chez nous* (op. 111) and *Vivons l'amour, vivons la vie* (op. 109); Marc-Cab
  on *C'est ton amour* (op. 112); Franz Waxman on seven cues for *Trouble
  for Two* (op. 131, 132, 135, 137–139 and 155, with Görner's list,
  `SRC0992`), *A Trader* (op. 155) among them since November 2026. For
  *The Next Morning*, *Second Deal* and *Suicide Club* (op. 137–139)
  Sieghardt names Jurmann and Waxman without Kaper, where the Jurmann
  catalogue and Görner name Kaper too; the archive keeps Kaper. Sieghardt
  also names Jurmann with Kaper on *My Heart Is a Gypsy* (op. 174), which
  stays `probable`, since the 1939 registration names Kaper alone. He also confirms Léo
  Lelièvre on *Vivons l'amour*, which is now cited. Paul Bertrand, Paul Henri
  Bertrand and Alexandre Tschesnokoff are named in the songs' notes rather than
  credited: no register holds them, and the archive does not make a person
  record from one book's mention.
- **MGM was claimant, Robbins publisher.** *You're All I Need* and *Love Song
  of Tahiti* credited MGM as publisher on their copyright entries, which
  name the claimant. They now credit MGM as claimant and Robbins Music
  Corporation as publisher. Robbins was first credited on Sieghardt
  (op. 121, op. 117); it now rests on documents of the time the archive
  already held, the Robbins edition of *You're All I Need* in the USC
  collection and Robbins's advertisement for *Love Song of Tahiti* in
  *Variety*, 27 November 1935.
- **A title variant.** Sieghardt titles *Es war einmal ein Baby* “Kinderlied”
  (op. 034), the refrain's first line in brackets; it is recorded as a
  variant.
- **The French version of Blue Venetian Waters** is *Sous le ciel de Venise*,
  as recorded in `docs/works-audit.md`.
- **One work catalogued as two.** Sieghardt calls *Signorina* an earlier text
  of *O Madonna* (op. 089), and the documents bear him out. Hofmeister, May
  1933, registers only two songs from *Ein Lied für dich*: *Ninon* and
  *Signorina! komm zu mir*, a 6/8 march-foxtrot with words by Marischka and
  Rotter; no *Madonna* edition appears. In the film (Internet Archive copy)
  and on Kiepura’s Odeon disc the tune is sung as *Oh Madonna!*. A disc by
  Carl Jörken (6108, coupled with *Ninon*) is titled *O signorina (O
  Madonna)* and sings the Madonna text. Kiepura’s Polish disc couples
  *Ninon* with *Signorina, signorina*, where the German one couples it with
  *Oh Madonna!*; the two recordings have the same form, and a chroma
  comparison of the audio aligns them far more closely than either with
  *Ninon*. The two records are now one Work, *Oh Madonna!* (`W-S078`), the
  title the film and the earliest disc give; *Signorina! komm zu mir* is its
  printed title (`TV0325`), and the Polish version is linked to it. The old
  address of `W-S068` leads to `W-S078`. Sieghardt’s Max Kolpe as author of
  the words is his alone: the disc labels and Hofmeister name Marischka and
  Rotter.
- **Pseudonyms.** Sieghardt gives *Trink dich gesund* (op. 093) as Jurmann’s,
  with Kaper, under the name Erich Walter Schmidt, with words by Rotter as
  Peter Schattner. The documents of 1933 print only those names: Hofmeister,
  June 1933 (`SRC0976`), registers a Schunkelwalzer by “Schmidt, Erich
  Walter” with words by “P. Schattner”, published by Edition Meisel, and
  Telefunken A 1450 (`SRC0977`, Deutsche Nationalbibliothek) credits “Erich
  Schmidt” and “Peter Schattner”. GEMA registers the same song, with the same
  publisher, under Jurmann, Kaper and Rotter. The names are recorded as a
  joint pseudonym of Kaper and Jurmann (`PNV0080`), as *Ernst Martin* is, and
  as Rotter’s (`PNV0081`); the credits give the printed names, and the genre
  is now the printed *Schunkelwalzer*.
- **Ich suche einen / eine.** Sieghardt’s *Ich suche eine, die mir allein
  gehört* (op. 054) is the title of the Ufaton edition, as Hofmeister
  registered it in June 1932. The film’s programme, *Illustrierter
  Film-Kurier* no. 1775 (`SRC0974`), prints the song as *Ich suche Einen, der
  mir allein gehört*, sung by the girls and by Evelyn: the same song in the
  woman’s and the man’s form. The archive keeps the film’s form as the title,
  now on the programme rather than a video upload, and the edition’s as a
  variant. The note no longer says that Willy Fritsch sang the man’s form in
  the film, which no document of the time states.

## Where the archive's documents outweigh Sieghardt

- *Ninon, ach uśmiechnij się!*: Sieghardt (op. 088/PL) lists a Polish
  version, *Malenka Ninon [Ninon, ach uśmiechnij]*, with words by Jan
  Wycislik. Jan Wyciślik is a present-day Katowice tenor who recorded *Ninon*
  on an undated charity cassette (`SRC0972`); the Biblioteka Narodowa and
  Discogs record him as its performer and name no author of the words, and no
  other source knows a Polish text by him. The first line Sieghardt quotes is
  that of Marcella Halicz's text of 1933, which Kiepura sang. The entry most
  likely takes the performer of a late recording for the author of the
  words. The song keeps Halicz, and its note says so.

- **Publishers**, checked against Hofmeister's monthly register (1931–1934),
  the United States copyright catalogues (1933–1935), the BnF catalogue and
  the Images Musicales collection of sheet-music covers. No document of the
  time confirms a publisher Sieghardt gives where the archive differs.
  - *Le plus petit baiser*: the cover of the edition is Choudens's (Images
    Musicales); Éditions Musicales Echo is Sieghardt's alone.
  - *À Paris tiguidiguidi*: no edition has been found anywhere; the archive
    names no publisher, and Echo is Sieghardt's alone.
  - *Adieu, es ist zu schön, um wahr zu sein* and *Wie schön ist diese
    Welt*: Hofmeister, November 1933, registers both with Dacapo-Verlag,
    Vienna, and has no Coda edition in 1933 or 1934. Coda published the
    French versions of the film's songs, which Sieghardt seems to have
    taken for the German.
  - The French *San Francisco*: the edition in the Jurmann collection at
    UCLA is France Mélodie's, 1936. Robbins, credited on Sieghardt alone,
    is no longer credited, and the song's note says so.
  - *Amanda*: the archive's Éditions musicales universelles rests on the
    Jurmann catalogue, a secondary source too, and Sieghardt gives Éditions
    Univers. With no edition found, the credit is `probable` and the note
    gives both.
- *La vie et l’amour*: Sieghardt makes it a work of its own (op. 075,
  German original unknown) and also lists Lucien Boyer’s French text under
  *Schade, daß Liebe ein Märchen ist* (op. 060/F). The Salabert edition of
  1932 in the BnF prints the German title beside the French: *La Vie et
  l’Amour (Schade, dass Liebe ein Märchen ist!)*. Boyer’s four French texts
  for the film each answer one German song, and Görner's list gives the
  French song Rotter’s words and Alrobi, Berlin, which are *Schade*’s.
  Op. 075 is *Schade* a second time; the archive keeps it as the French
  version. Réda Caire’s Pathé disc of 1933 (`SRC0975`) records it under the
  film’s French title.
- **Publishers resting on Sieghardt alone**, checked against Hofmeister
  (1931–1938), the United States copyright catalogues (1936–1937) and the
  BnF. None is confirmed by a document of the time, and each is now
  `probable`, with its place in Sieghardt named.
  - *Les charmes chantent, mon violon soupire*: Sieghardt gives Edition Karl
    Brüll and Éditions Salabert (op. 092/F, p. 194); the BnF, which holds
    Salabert’s other Jurmann editions of 1931–1934, has none of it. The
    credit had rested on the Jurmann catalogue, which names no publisher.
  - *Blaue venezianische Wasser* and *Sous le ciel de Venise*: Sieghardt
    gives Robbins Music Corporation (op. 128/A and 128/F, p. 198), the
    publisher of the English original. The 1937 catalogue registers only
    the English song with Robbins, and Hofmeister lists no work by Kaper or
    Jurmann in 1936–1938.
  - *Tous les noirs* and *Demain matin tout ira mieux*: Robbins as for the
    English originals, which the 1937 catalogue registers with Robbins; no
    edition of the French versions has been found. MGM, credited as a second
    publisher of *Demain matin*, rested on nothing and is no longer credited.
  - *Wir sind international*: no document of the time names the song at
    all. Hofmeister’s registrations of 1931 list five songs from *Die große
    Attraktion*, by Kaper, Brodszky and Lehár, and not this one. Sieghardt
    does not name it either: his filmography (no. 9, p. 214) lists Kaper
    among the film's composers but gives as its music only Jurmann’s
    *Parlez-vous français?* (op. 041, Alrobi 1931), and the title is in
    neither his opus list nor Görner's list. The song and
    its credits are now `probable`. It entered the archive from the
    earliest version of the site, with no document; `SRC0373`, its only
    source, does not contain it.
- *On n'aime qu'une fois*: the United States copyright registration of 1935
  (`SRC0973`) names André de Badet alone as author of the words, where
  Sieghardt adds Paul Henri Bertrand. The song's note says so.
- *Un regard de femme* and *Dairy-Maid Song*: Sieghardt credits Jurmann
  alone; the BnF catalogue and the 1936 copyright registration credit Kaper
  and Jurmann.
- **Years.** Sieghardt dates seven songs differently from the archive, from
  the publication years he reports. Under `docs/work-years.md` his catalogue
  does not date a Work, and no document of the time supports his year in any
  of the seven. The archive keeps its years:

- *Falling in Love with You*, *Learn to Smile* and *The Sweetest Song in All
  the World*: Sieghardt (op. 187, 189 and 191, p. 203) and Görner date
  them 1941, from later editions; their copyright registrations date them
  1932 and 1934.
- *Celui que j’aime*: Sieghardt gives Leo Feist, 1937 (op. 156/F); the
  France-Mélodie edition of 1939 (`SRC0303`) stands.

- **Films.**
  - *My Song for You*: Sieghardt names Spoliansky, Kaper and Jurmann for its
    music and says *Ninon* was sung in it in English. *The Box Office
    Check-Up of 1935* (`SRC0978`) lists the film’s songs as Spoliansky and
    Eyton’s *My Song for You* and *With All My Heart*, with pieces by Verdi
    and Schubert; the English *Ninon* registered in 1933 is from *A Song for
    You*, the British title of *Ein Lied für Dich*. The archive gives the film
    no music credit for Kaper, and its note no longer calls the use of
    *Ninon* documented.
  - *Wings of the Morning*: Kaper and Jurmann were credited `confirmed` on
    IMDb alone, for a march from *Les nuits moscovites* heard uncredited. The
    copyright registrations (`SRC0979`) name Muir Mathieson as music director
    and the *Hollywood Spectator* review (`SRC0980`) Arthur Benjamin for the
    music; the Jurmann catalogue and Görner's list record the cues Jurmann
    and Kaper wrote for the film as not used, and Sieghardt's filmography
    names Arthur Benjamin and, with a question mark, Jurmann. The two credits are now
    `probable`, and the note says who gives what.
  - *Mariage à responsabilité limitée*: André Mauprey was credited as a
    composer on Unifrance. Sieghardt gives him the French film’s lyrics, and
    the Eschig editions of 1932 print his French words for the songs of the
    German original; he is now credited as its lyricist.
  - *Little Boy Blue* (`F052`): two films of 1936 have the title. The
    archive recorded Ub Iwerks’s ComiColor cartoon, released by Celebrity
    Productions on 30 July 1936 (`SRC0985`). Sieghardt’s filmography (p. 226)
    describes the other, MGM’s one-reel Miniature after Eugene Field’s poem,
    which the copyright registration (`SRC0983`, MP6493) gives to Harold S.
    Bucquet as director and MGM’s schedule (`SRC0984`) releases on 23 May
    1936. His release date, 30 July 1936, is the cartoon's, and Görner's
    list and the Jurmann catalogue call the film animated. The earlier note
    that Sieghardt adds Carl W. Stalling was wrong: his entry names only
    Kaper and Jurmann for the music. Kaper and
    Jurmann were MGM’s composers that year, so the record now describes the
    Miniature: Bucquet and MGM confirmed, Jack Chertok as producer and the
    music `probable` on Sieghardt and the Jurmann catalogue, which no
    document of the time confirms. Iwerks, Stalling, Celebrity Productions,
    the cartoon’s poster and the Iwerks photograph are withdrawn, and their
    pages lead to the film.
  - *Kind, ich freu’ mich auf Dein Kommen*: Sieghardt has Gerron alone as
    director; filmportal names Kurt Gerron and Erich von Neusser, who was also
    head of production. The sheet-music cover names no director, and no
    document of the time was found that settles it, so the archive keeps
    both.
  - **Filmography entries 1–16** (pp. 212–216). *Ihre Majestät die Liebe*
    (no. 3) and its French and American versions (nos. 4 and 10) name
    Jurmann and Willy Schmidt-Gentner for the music and not Kaper, whose
    credit on the film rests on IMDb alone and stays `probable`; the film’s
    note now says so. *Ehe m.b.H.* (no. 11) adds Hisa-Film and Sam Spiegel
    as producers, which no document in the archive gives; they are not
    added. *Die Zwei vom Südexpreß* (no. 16) names Friedrich Jung, Hans
    Wehning and Leonhard Fürst for the background music and Kaper and
    Jurmann for op. 064, *Solang’ wir zwei nur gute Freunde sind*, as the
    archive does. *Es wird schon wieder besser*, *Skandal in der
    Parkstraße*, *Melodie der Liebe* and *Ein toller Einfall* (nos. 12–15)
    agree with the archive too.
  - **Filmography entries 17–20** (p. 217) agree with the archive. For
    *Hochzeitsreise zu Dritt* Sieghardt names Karl Meinhardt as director,
    where the archive, on a film database and an advertisement of the time, has Erich Schmidt
    and Carl Meinhard as dialogue director; his op. 069 for the film is a
    Swedish foxtrot and is most likely a misprint for op. 079, *Wenn ich
    ’mal eine Dummheit mach’*.

## Left for decision or further research

- **Songs the archive did not hold.** Sieghardt credits Kaper on three songs
  that were not in the archive. For two of them the documents of the time
  name Jurmann without Kaper:
  - *And Still I Love You* (op. 016): the 1930 United States registration
    gives “words by Irving Caesar, music by Walter Jurmann, new arr. by
    Albert Szirmai” (Harms, © 23 October 1930). Sieghardt’s opus list adds
    Kaper; Görner's list does not.
  - *Am schönen Titicacasee* (op. 021): Hofmeister enters it in 1930 under
    “Rotter, Fritz und Walter Jurmann” (Wiener Boheme-Verlag) and in 1932 as
    “[Rotter u. Jurmann]”, and the 1930 United States registration gives
    “Text u. Musik von Fritz Rotter u. Walter Jurmann”. No Italian or
    American version was found.
  Neither is added.
  The third, *Melodie der Liebe*, the film’s title song (op. 057), is
  documented. Hofmeister’s 1932 entry for the film’s song book lists four
  songs and not this one, but the 1932 United States catalogue registers it
  (`SRC0986`): “Melodie der liebe; schlager, w Fritz Rotter, m B. Kaper u.
  Walter Jurmann. © Apr. 25, 1932; Alrobi”. It is added (`W-S230`), linked
  to the film as `probable`, since only Sieghardt names the film: his
  filmography (no. 14, p. 216) has op. 057 sung by Richard Tauber in it.
- **Language versions.** The versions Sieghardt lists under the opus numbers
  of songs the archive holds were checked in the United States copyright
  catalogues (1930–1937), Hofmeister (1929–1938), the BnF, the Deutsche
  Nationalbibliothek, LIBRIS, the Internet Archive and Discogs, and in
  November 2026 in the Italian union catalogue (OPAC SBN), the Royal Danish
  Library, the Finnish Finna, the National Library of Norway and the Dutch
  national library with its newspaper archive, Delpher. A version is added
  only where a document of the time ties it to the original: the same title
  and film, the original title printed beside it, or the original words
  acknowledged.
  - Added: the English *Ninon* by Harry S. Pepper (`W-S231`; registered 22
    August 1933, Dix, Ltd.); the Swedish *Ninon* by Arne Olovson (`W-S232`;
    Carl Gehrmans’s print in LIBRIS and two Swedish discs of 1933); the
    Swedish version of *Du lieber Geiger, spiel einen Tango für mich*, *Å,
    denna tango*, also by Olovson (`W-S233`; Gehrmans, [1931]), which
    Sieghardt does not list; the Italian *Ninon* by Michele Galdieri
    (`W-S234`; Curci, c1933, from “Aspetto una signora”, the Italian release
    of *Ein Lied für Dich*); the Italian *Tu sei l’amor* by Alfredo Bracchi
    (`W-S235`; Curci, ©1935, and a Columbia disc titled “Tu sei l’amore =
    You’re all I need”); the Italian *San Francisco* by Galdieri (`W-S236`;
    Curci, ©1937); the Danish *Ninon, smil engang endnu* by Ahl-Nielsen
    (`W-S237`; Alfred Thorsing, 1933, and three Danish discs of 1933); and
    the Danish and Swedish *Oh Madonna*, by Giovanni di Sorrento and Per
    Silja (`W-S238`, `W-S239`), printed together in Thorsing’s edition of
    1933, which gives the German song as *Signorina*.
  - Found in documents of the time but not added, because no document says
    which original they translate: in OPAC SBN, *Domani chissà* and *Segui
    il ritmo* (Bracchi; Curci, 1937 and 1938), from *A Day at the Races*;
    *Canta il mio cuore* and *Il primo amore* (Lulli; Franchi, Rome, ©1936
    and 1937), from *Three Smart Girls*; *Signorinetta* (Bosio) and *Se
    vuoi!* (“N.M.”), from *Viaggio di nozze in tre*, the Italian release of
    *Hochzeitsreise zu Dritt*, on a Columbia disc of [1933?]; *Partir*
    (Luciano Ramo; Suvini-Zerboni, 1934), from *Un’avventura al Lido*; *Bel
    gorilla*, a paso doble for small orchestra (Suvini-Zerboni, 1934); and
    *Danubiana*, a foxtrot “di F. Rotter e B. Kaper” (Suvini-Zerboni, 1932).
    In the Royal Danish Library, *Hvem vil for en Kvinde græde?* (Jac.
    Boesen, 1932), parts for salon orchestra, which Sieghardt gives as the
    Danish *Wer weint heut’ aus Liebe Tränen?* (op. 046). OPAC SBN also
    records *La prima sensazione* and *Quando piango son felice* on undated
    discs.
  - Already in the archive: the French *Sport (Nur Sport)*, *La vie et
    l’amour*, *Dit’s-moi, Madame*, *Ça va déjà mieux* and both texts of
    *Ninon, quand tu me souris*; Harold Adamson’s *Ninon*; *Love Is a Fairy
    Tale*.
  - Known only from Sieghardt, with no document found: *Ninon* in Czech,
    Hungarian, Norwegian, Dutch (*Zonder jou*), Brazilian and Finnish (*Jos
    jäät*); the Italian *Senza amore*, *Tu mi piaci*, *Notte sul mare* and
    the Italian *Ein Lied aus meiner Heimat*; the German *Cosi Cosa*
    (Günther Behrle), *San Francisco, du bist die Stadt für mich* (Rotter),
    *Natascha* and *Die Liebe kommt*; the French *Cosi Cosa* (Secchia and
    Vella), *Happy New Year* (Isabelle Kloutchkowski) and *Schön ist das ganze
    Leben*; the Dutch *Signorina*, *Madonna* and *Nur Sport*; the Greek
    *Signorina*; the Hungarian *San Francisco*; the English *My Heart Was

    instrumental or sung in English. The Czech, Hungarian and Brazilian
    national catalogues could not be searched from here (the Czech union
    catalogue turns automated requests away), so those stay open rather than
    disproved.
