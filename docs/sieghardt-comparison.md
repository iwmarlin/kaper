# The archive against Sieghardt's work catalogue, 8 November 2026

Alexander Sieghardt's catalogue of Walter Jurmann's works, in Elisabeth
Buxbaum's *„Veronika, der Lenz ist da“* (2006; `SRC0373`), was read in full
for this comparison: the opus list (pp. 178–199, op. 001–135), the
year-by-year list (pp. 200–217) and the filmography (pp. 218–231). Every
entry that names Kaper was compared with the archive's Work, credits, titles
and dates.

Sieghardt is a secondary source, and not always consistent with himself:
the opus list and the year-by-year list give *Hi-di-ho* and *Hi-do-ho*, *Le
bistro* and *Le bistrot du port*, *Sous le ciel de Venise* and *Venice*.
Where the archive holds a primary document that disagrees with him, the
document stands, and that is noted below.

## Done

- **Sieghardt was cited for what he does not say.** *Chanson d'amour de
  Tahiti* credited Ch. Davson on Sieghardt; he names Charles Secchia and
  Jean Vella (op. 117/F). Davson now rests on the France-Mélodie edition
  alone, and the song's note gives Sieghardt's attribution. *Pourquoi donc
  verser des larmes* credited André Mauprey on Sieghardt, whose opus list
  says “unknown”; his filmography, though, credits the French film's lyrics,
  this song among them, to Mauprey (p. 221). The credit was kept as
  `probable`; it is now confirmed by the Eschig voice-and-piano edition of
  1932 in the BnF (`SRC0981`), which prints “Paroles françaises de André
  Mauprey”.
- **Authors Sieghardt adds**, credited `probable` on his catalogue alone:
  Ernst Marischka on *Ninon, lach mir einmal zu* (op. 088); Henri-Georges
  Clouzot and Serge Veber on *O Madona!* (op. 089/F); Louis Poterat on *C'est
  chez nous* (op. 111) and *Vivons l'amour, vivons la vie* (op. 109); Marc-Cab
  on *C'est ton amour* (op. 112); Franz Waxman on six cues for *Trouble for
  Two* (op. 131, 132, 135 and the year-by-year list). He also confirms Léo
  Lelièvre on *Vivons l'amour*, which is now cited. Paul Bertrand, Paul Henri
  Bertrand and Alexandre Tschesnokoff are named in the songs' notes rather than
  credited: no register holds them, and the archive does not make a person
  record from one book's mention.
- **MGM was claimant, Robbins publisher.** *You're All I Need* and *Love Song
  of Tahiti* credited MGM as publisher on their copyright entries, which
  name the claimant. They now credit MGM as claimant and Robbins Music
  Corporation as publisher. Robbins was first credited on Sieghardt
  (op. 121, op. 117); it now rests on documents of the time the archive
  already held, the Robbins edition of *You're All I Need* in the USC
  collection and Robbins's advertisement for *Love Song of Tahiti* in
  *Variety*, 27 November 1935.
- **A title variant.** Sieghardt titles *Es war einmal ein Baby* “Kinderlied”
  (op. 034), the refrain's first line in brackets; it is recorded as a
  variant.
- **The French version of Blue Venetian Waters** is *Sous le ciel de Venise*,
  as recorded in `docs/works-audit.md`.
- **One work catalogued as two.** Sieghardt calls *Signorina* an earlier text
  of *O Madonna* (op. 089), and the documents bear him out. Hofmeister, May
  1933, registers only two songs from *Ein Lied für dich*: *Ninon* and
  *Signorina! komm zu mir*, a 6/8 march-foxtrot with words by Marischka and
  Rotter; no *Madonna* edition appears. In the film (Internet Archive copy)
  and on Kiepura’s Odeon disc the tune is sung as *Oh Madonna!*. A disc by
  Carl Jörken (6108, coupled with *Ninon*) is titled *O signorina (O
  Madonna)* and sings the Madonna text. Kiepura’s Polish disc couples
  *Ninon* with *Signorina, signorina*, where the German one couples it with
  *Oh Madonna!*; the two recordings have the same form, and a chroma
  comparison of the audio aligns them far more closely than either with
  *Ninon*. The two records are now one Work, *Oh Madonna!* (`W-S078`), the
  title the film and the earliest disc give; *Signorina! komm zu mir* is its
  printed title (`TV0325`), and the Polish version is linked to it. The old
  address of `W-S068` leads to `W-S078`. Sieghardt’s Max Kolpe as author of
  the words is his alone: the disc labels and Hofmeister name Marischka and
  Rotter.
- **Pseudonyms.** Sieghardt gives *Trink dich gesund* as Jurmann’s,
  with Kaper, under the name Erich Walter Schmidt, with words by Rotter as
  Peter Schattner. The documents of 1933 print only those names: Hofmeister,
  June 1933 (`SRC0976`), registers a Schunkelwalzer by “Schmidt, Erich
  Walter” with words by “P. Schattner”, published by Edition Meisel, and
  Telefunken A 1450 (`SRC0977`, Deutsche Nationalbibliothek) credits “Erich
  Schmidt” and “Peter Schattner”. GEMA registers the same song, with the same
  publisher, under Jurmann, Kaper and Rotter. The names are recorded as a
  joint pseudonym of Kaper and Jurmann (`PNV0080`), as *Ernst Martin* is, and
  as Rotter’s (`PNV0081`); the credits give the printed names, and the genre
  is now the printed *Schunkelwalzer*.
- **Ich suche einen / eine.** Sieghardt’s *Ich suche eine, die mir allein
  gehört* (op. 054) is the title of the Ufaton edition, as Hofmeister
  registered it in June 1932. The film’s programme, *Illustrierter
  Film-Kurier* no. 1775 (`SRC0974`), prints the song as *Ich suche Einen, der
  mir allein gehört*, sung by the girls and by Evelyn: the same song in the
  woman’s and the man’s form. The archive keeps the film’s form as the title,
  now on the programme rather than a video upload, and the edition’s as a
  variant. The note no longer says that Willy Fritsch sang the man’s form in
  the film, which no document of the time states.

## Where the archive's documents outweigh Sieghardt

- *Ninon, ach uśmiechnij się!*: Sieghardt (op. 088/PL) lists a Polish
  version, *Malenka Ninon [Ninon, ach uśmiechnij]*, with words by Jan
  Wycislik. Jan Wyciślik is a present-day Katowice tenor who recorded *Ninon*
  on an undated charity cassette (`SRC0972`); the Biblioteka Narodowa and
  Discogs record him as its performer and name no author of the words, and no
  other source knows a Polish text by him. The first line Sieghardt quotes is
  that of Marcella Halicz's text of 1933, which Kiepura sang. The entry most
  likely takes the performer of a late recording for the author of the
  words. The song keeps Halicz, and its note says so.

- **Publishers**, checked against Hofmeister's monthly register (1931–1934),
  the United States copyright catalogues (1933–1935), the BnF catalogue and
  the Images Musicales collection of sheet-music covers. No document of the
  time confirms a publisher Sieghardt gives where the archive differs.
  - *Le plus petit baiser*: the cover of the edition is Choudens's (Images
    Musicales); Éditions Musicales Echo is Sieghardt's alone.
  - *À Paris tiguidiguidi*: no edition has been found anywhere; the archive
    names no publisher, and Echo is Sieghardt's alone.
  - *Adieu, es ist zu schön, um wahr zu sein* and *Wie schön ist diese
    Welt*: Hofmeister, November 1933, registers both with Dacapo-Verlag,
    Vienna, and has no Coda edition in 1933 or 1934. Coda published the
    French versions of the film's songs, which Sieghardt seems to have
    taken for the German.
  - The French *San Francisco*: the edition in the Jurmann collection at
    UCLA is France Mélodie's, 1936. Robbins, credited on Sieghardt alone,
    is no longer credited, and the song's note says so.
  - *Amanda*: the archive's Éditions musicales universelles rests on the
    Jurmann catalogue, a secondary source too, and Sieghardt gives Éditions
    Univers. With no edition found, the credit is `probable` and the note
    gives both.
- *La vie et l’amour*: Sieghardt makes it a work of its own (op. 075,
  German original unknown) and also lists Lucien Boyer’s French text under
  *Schade, daß Liebe ein Märchen ist* (op. 060/F). The Salabert edition of
  1932 in the BnF prints the German title beside the French: *La Vie et
  l’Amour (Schade, dass Liebe ein Märchen ist!)*. Boyer’s four French texts
  for the film each answer one German song, and his year-by-year list gives
  the French song Rotter’s words and Alrobi, Berlin, which are *Schade*’s.
  Op. 075 is *Schade* a second time; the archive keeps it as the French
  version. Réda Caire’s Pathé disc of 1933 (`SRC0975`) records it under the
  film’s French title.
- *On n'aime qu'une fois*: the United States copyright registration of 1935
  (`SRC0973`) names André de Badet alone as author of the words, where
  Sieghardt adds Paul Henri Bertrand. The song's note says so.
- *Un regard de femme* and *Dairy-Maid Song*: Sieghardt credits Jurmann
  alone; the BnF catalogue and the 1936 copyright registration credit Kaper
  and Jurmann.
- **Years.** Sieghardt dates seven songs differently from the archive, from
  the publication years he reports. Under `docs/work-years.md` his catalogue
  does not date a Work, and no document of the time supports his year in any
  of the seven. The archive keeps its years:

  | Work | Sieghardt | Archive | Earliest document |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | *Wer weint heut’ aus Liebe Tränen?* | 1931 | 1932 | Hofmeister 1932; none in Hofmeister 1931 |
  | *Ein Lied aus meiner Heimat* | 1931 | 1932 | the same |
  | *Es war einmal ein Baby* | 1931 | 1932 | the same |

- *Falling in Love with You*, *Learn to Smile* and *The Sweetest Song in All
  the World*: Sieghardt dates them 1941, from later editions; their copyright
  registrations date them 1932 and 1934.

- **Films.**
  - *My Song for You*: Sieghardt names Spoliansky, Kaper and Jurmann for its
    music and says *Ninon* was sung in it in English. *The Box Office
    Check-Up of 1935* (`SRC0978`) lists the film’s songs as Spoliansky and
    Eyton’s *My Song for You* and *With All My Heart*, with pieces by Verdi
    and Schubert; the English *Ninon* registered in 1933 is from *A Song for
    You*, the British title of *Ein Lied für Dich*. The archive gives the film
    no music credit for Kaper, and its note no longer calls the use of
    *Ninon* documented.
  - *Wings of the Morning*: Kaper and Jurmann were credited `confirmed` on
    IMDb alone, for a march from *Les nuits moscovites* heard uncredited. The
    copyright registrations (`SRC0979`) name Muir Mathieson as music director
    and the *Hollywood Spectator* review (`SRC0980`) Arthur Benjamin for the
    music; Sieghardt and the Jurmann catalogue record the cues Jurmann and
    Kaper wrote for the film as not used. The two credits are now
    `probable`, and the note says who gives what.
  - *Mariage à responsabilité limitée*: André Mauprey was credited as a
    composer on Unifrance. Sieghardt gives him the French film’s lyrics, and
    the Eschig editions of 1932 print his French words for the songs of the
    German original; he is now credited as its lyricist.
  - *Little Boy Blue* (`F052`): two films of 1936 have the title. The
    archive recorded Ub Iwerks’s ComiColor cartoon, released by Celebrity
    Productions on 30 July 1936 (`SRC0985`). Sieghardt’s filmography (p. 226)
    describes the other, MGM’s one-reel Miniature after Eugene Field’s poem,
    which the copyright registration (`SRC0983`, MP6493) gives to Harold S.
    Bucquet as director and MGM’s schedule (`SRC0984`) releases on 23 May
    1936; his year-by-year list and the Jurmann catalogue call it animated,
    and he adds Carl W. Stalling, which belong to the cartoon. Kaper and
    Jurmann were MGM’s composers that year, so the record now describes the
    Miniature: Bucquet and MGM confirmed, Jack Chertok as producer and the
    music `probable` on Sieghardt and the Jurmann catalogue, which no
    document of the time confirms. Iwerks, Stalling, Celebrity Productions,
    the cartoon’s poster and the Iwerks photograph are withdrawn, and their
    pages lead to the film.
  - *Kind, ich freu’ mich auf Dein Kommen*: Sieghardt has Gerron alone as
    director; filmportal names Kurt Gerron and Erich von Neusser, who was also
    head of production. The sheet-music cover names no director, and no
    document of the time was found that settles it, so the archive keeps
    both.

## Left for decision or further research

- **Publisher credits resting on Sieghardt or the Jurmann catalogue alone**,
  to be checked the same way: *Wir sind international* (Alrobi; Hofmeister
  1931 has the film's other songs with Alrobi, not this one), *Les charmes
  chantent* (Salabert), and Robbins on *Blaue venezianische Wasser*, *Sous le
  ciel de Venise*, *Tous les noirs* and *Demain matin tout ira mieux*, with MGM
  on the last. Four of these versions have no Source but Sieghardt.
- **Not in the archive.** *And Still I Love You* (op. 016), *Am schönen
  Titicacasee* (op. 021) and the title song of *Melodie der Liebe* (op. 057);
  the Italian version of *Ein Lied für Dich*, *Aspetto una signorina*; and
  about thirty-five language versions Sieghardt lists under the opus numbers
  of songs the archive holds.
