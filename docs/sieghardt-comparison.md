# The archive against Sieghardt's work catalogue, 8 November 2026

Alexander Sieghardt's catalogue of Walter Jurmann's works, in Elisabeth
Buxbaum's *„Veronika, der Lenz ist da“* (2006; `SRC0373`), was read in full
for this comparison: the opus list (pp. 178–199, op. 001–135), the
year-by-year list (pp. 200–217) and the filmography (pp. 218–231). Every
entry that names Kaper was compared with the archive's Work, credits, titles
and dates.

Sieghardt is a secondary source, and not always consistent with himself:
the opus list and the year-by-year list give *Hi-di-ho* and *Hi-do-ho*, *Le
bistro* and *Le bistrot du port*, *Sous le ciel de Venise* and *Venice*.
Where the archive holds a primary document that disagrees with him, the
document stands, and that is noted below.

## Done

- **Sieghardt was cited for what he does not say.** *Chanson d'amour de
  Tahiti* credited Ch. Davson on Sieghardt; he names Charles Secchia and
  Jean Vella (op. 117/F). Davson now rests on the France-Mélodie edition
  alone, and the song's note gives Sieghardt's attribution. *Pourquoi donc
  verser des larmes* credited André Mauprey on Sieghardt, whose opus list
  says “unknown”; his filmography, though, credits the French film's lyrics,
  this song among them, to Mauprey (p. 221). The credit stays, `probable`,
  with both places named.
- **Authors Sieghardt adds**, credited `probable` on his catalogue alone:
  Ernst Marischka on *Ninon, lach mir einmal zu* (op. 088); Henri-Georges
  Clouzot and Serge Veber on *O Madona!* (op. 089/F); Louis Poterat on *C'est
  chez nous* (op. 111) and *Vivons l'amour, vivons la vie* (op. 109); Marc-Cab
  on *C'est ton amour* (op. 112); Franz Waxman on six cues for *Trouble for
  Two* (op. 131, 132, 135 and the year-by-year list). He also confirms Léo
  Lelièvre on *Vivons l'amour*, which is now cited. Paul Bertrand, Paul Henri
  Bertrand and Alexandre Tschesnokoff are named in the songs' notes rather than
  credited: no register holds them, and the archive does not make a person
  record from one book's mention.
- **MGM was claimant, Robbins publisher.** *You're All I Need* and *Love Song
  of Tahiti* credited MGM as publisher on their copyright entries, which
  name the claimant. They now credit MGM as claimant and Robbins Music
  Corporation as publisher, on Sieghardt (op. 121, op. 117).
- **A title variant.** Sieghardt titles *Es war einmal ein Baby* “Kinderlied”
  (op. 034), the refrain's first line in brackets; it is recorded as a
  variant.
- **The French version of Blue Venetian Waters** is *Sous le ciel de Venise*,
  as recorded in `docs/works-audit.md`.

## Where the archive's documents outweigh Sieghardt

- *Un regard de femme* and *Dairy-Maid Song*: Sieghardt credits Jurmann
  alone; the BnF catalogue and the 1936 copyright registration credit Kaper
  and Jurmann.
- *Falling in Love with You*, *Learn to Smile* and *The Sweetest Song in All
  the World*: Sieghardt dates them 1941, from later editions; their copyright
  registrations date them 1932 and 1934.

## Left for decision or further research

- **Little Boy Blue** (`F052`). The archive records Ub Iwerks's cartoon;
  Sieghardt's filmography describes an MGM one-reel miniature of 1936 by
  Harold S. Bucquet, produced by Jack Chertok, with Chic Sale, and his
  year-by-year list calls it an animated short. The film may be the wrong one.
- **Years** Sieghardt gives earlier than the archive, from publication years
  he reports: *Wer weint heut' aus Liebe Tränen* and *Ein Lied aus meiner
  Heimat* (1931), *Es war einmal ein Baby* and *Once There Was a Baby*
  (1931), *Nur Sport!* (1932), *Le bistrot du port* (1933); *Tränen in der
  Geige* the other way (1933 against 1932). Whether a secondary source's
  publication year can date a Work is for `docs/work-years.md` to say.
- **Identity.** Sieghardt makes *Vie et l'amour* a work of its own (op. 075),
  where the archive makes it the French version of *Schade, daß Liebe ein
  Märchen ist*; and he calls *Signorina* an earlier text of *O Madonna*
  (op. 089), where the archive has two Works.
- **Titles.** *Ich suche einen, der mir allein gehört* is *Ich suche eine,
  die mir allein gehört* in Sieghardt (op. 054).
- **Publishers.** *Le plus petit baiser* and *À Paris tiguidiguidi*: Éditions
  Musicales Echo (1933) in Sieghardt, Choudens or none in the archive; *Adieu,
  es ist zu schön, um wahr zu sein* and *Wie schön ist diese Welt*: Edition
  Coda in Sieghardt, Dacapo only in the archive; *Amanda*: “Editions Univers”;
  the French *San Francisco*: Francis Day, Paris.
- **Other credits.** The Polish *Ninon*: Sieghardt has *Malenka Ninon* with
  words by Jan Wycislik, the BN disc Marcella Halicz. *Trink dich gesund*:
  Sieghardt gives the pseudonyms Erich Walter Schmidt (Jurmann) and Peter
  Schattner (Rotter).
- **Films.** *My Song for You* has no music credit in the archive, where
  Sieghardt names Spoliansky, Kaper and Jurmann; *Wings of the Morning*: his
  filmography has “Arthur Benjamin & Walter Jurmann?”, his year-by-year list
  Kaper and Jurmann cues “nicht verwendet”; *Mariage à responsabilité
  limitée*: Mauprey wrote the lyrics, where the archive lists him among the
  music credits; *Kind, ich freu' mich auf Dein Kommen*: Gerron alone directs
  in Sieghardt.
- **Not in the archive.** *And Still I Love You* (op. 016), *Am schönen
  Titicacasee* (op. 021) and the title song of *Melodie der Liebe* (op. 057);
  the Italian version of *Ein Lied für Dich*, *Aspetto una signorina*; and
  about thirty-five language versions Sieghardt lists under the opus numbers
  of songs the archive holds.
