# Timeline audit, 9 November 2026

The 56 Timeline Events were read against the Sources they cite and against
the documents of the time the archive already holds. Most rest on archival
documents (the University of Warsaw student file, the baptism entry, the
passenger manifests and naturalisation papers, the Kaper Papers), on press
items of the time, or on Iwona Lindstedt's two source-based articles. Eight
cited no document of the time; five of those now do, and one was rewritten.

## Corrected

- **`TE0042` End of the Jurmann–Kaper partnership.** It was dated 1938 and
  said the collaboration “ended amicably as changes in the film industry
  reduced demand for thematic songs”, citing only Sieghardt's catalogue,
  which is a work list and says nothing of the kind. The date is now 1939:
  the last music registered in both names is “At night”, the cue for *The
  Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* written with Franz Waxman and registered
  on 25 February 1939 (`SRC0280`). The two later joint songs that Sieghardt
  and Görner list, *Harbour Café* (1941) and the unused *Blush and Sigh*,
  are named as theirs alone, and the event no longer states a reason for
  the end of the partnership, since no document in the archive gives one.
- **`TE0037` Hollywood debut.** “He supplied a single number, ‘You're All I
  Need’, to words by Gus Kahn” now names Jurmann beside Kaper and says that
  the AFI catalogue (`SRC0140`, now cited) adds Harold Adamson, as the
  song's own credits do.

## Given documents of the time

- **`TE0015` Move to Berlin** rested on Lindstedt alone. The Kaper Papers
  date it: the *Piece for Orchestra* of 1926 is headed “written in Warsaw”
  (`SRC0009`) and *Hätt' ich einen Kammerdiener?* of 1926 is listed as
  written in Berlin (`SRC0959`). The oral history (`SRC0494`) is cited for
  his intention to enrol at the Hochschule.
- **`TE0021` Early publications with Fritz Rotter** now cites Hofmeister's
  register for December 1929 (`SRC0102`, `SRC0103`), which lists a valse
  Boston published in Vienna and a tango published by Seifert, the “German
  and Viennese editions” the event describes.
- **`TE0027` Flourishing Berlin collaboration** now cites the
  advertisements and programme for the films it names (`SRC0026`,
  `SRC0109`, `SRC0333`) and the advertisements for *Der glückliche Herr
  Fünf* (August 1932, `SRC0690`) and *Ich will dich Liebe lehren* (1933,
  `SRC0416`), which bear out its last sentence.
- **`TE0029` Paris period** now cites the S.S. Champlain manifest
  (`SRC0944`) for the Paris address Kaper gave in New York.

## Checked and left

- **`TE0002`, `TE0051`, `TE0057`** cite periodical articles, but among them
  are documents of the time printed as articles: the Staszic school's report
  for 1915/16 (`SRC0948`) and the Fourth Ordinance on the Exhibition of
  Foreign Films (`SRC0946`). The rest are studies of the period, which is
  what these events describe.
- **`TE0043` Kaper's solo career** rests on his 1975 interview in
  *Soundtrack Magazine* (`SRC0864`) for his own account of the move from
  songs to scores, which is what it states.
- **`TE0029`** attributes a judgement to Matthias Brzoska and quotes Jacques
  Ibert's review in *Le Matin*; neither is a Source of its own, and both
  reach the archive through Lindstedt (`SRC0491`), which the event cites.
- **`TE0047`** gives Kaper's own account of “Henry Craig” and Andrzej Włast
  through Lindstedt (`SRC0490`); the account itself is not cited directly.

## Images, 14 November 2026

Once restricted images were kept out of the chronology, 25 events had no
image left. Each was given one whose rights allow it to be featured. 55 of
56 events now have one.

- **Eighteen** come from media the archive already held. Among them:
  - the *Radjo* listing of the Bick–Kaper duo (`TE0019`);
  - the *Zum 5 Uhr-Tee* album advertisement with “Gib nur acht” (`TE0021`);
  - the 1932 Berlin address-book entry (`TE0027`);
  - the Kiepura reception photograph with the Kapers (`TE0029`);
  - the Escapade clipping (`TE0037`).

permission to publish was given to the library alone. `TE0045`, the police
