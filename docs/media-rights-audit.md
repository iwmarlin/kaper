# Media rights audit, 12 November 2026

All 410 Media records were read for their rights status, rights note and
credit line. The reasoning in most notes is careful. They work through the
source-country term, the United States restoration date of 1 January 1996
(URAA), notice and renewal, and the Polish rule that left unreserved
photographs unprotected.

The archive's practice can be stated as two rules. They are not written
down anywhere else:

- **Public domain** means free in the United States, where the site is
  hosted, and in the source country.
- **Restricted** and **copyright undetermined** files may be hosted only on
  detail pages (`galleryStatus: detail_only`), for identification and
  commentary.

The findings below are the places where records depart from those two
rules.

## 1. Five audio files publish Kaper's music without a recorded permission

`M010`–`M014` are renderings by Iwona Lindstedt of *Rozmowa*, the *Etude*
and the three movements of the *Sonatina*, made from the manuscripts in the
Kaper Papers. They run from 1:13 to 4:17 and present each piece complete.

The notes clear the recording and say plainly that the music is not
cleared. Kaper died in 1983, so the compositions are protected in Poland and
the European Union until the end of 2053. They were never published, so
the United States term is also life plus seventy years, to the same date.

No permission from the estate, and no other legal basis, is recorded. These
are the only files in the archive that reproduce a protected work whole,
and they are gallery-selected.

**Decision needed:** record a permission if one exists; otherwise shorten
the files to excerpts used for analysis, or withdraw them.

## 2. Two records are marked public domain against the archive's own reasoning

- **`M461` Jean Féline, Excelsior, 25 June 1932.** The note says the French
  term had expired before 1 January 1996. It had not: an anonymous work of
  1932 ran seventy years to the end of 2002. The URAA therefore restored
  United States copyright to the end of 2027. `M433`, from the same
  newspaper in 1936, is correctly marked restricted on exactly this
  reasoning.
- **`M319` Pierre Billon, 1937 press-clipping page.** The record relies on
  the Commons PD-France tag. An anonymous French press photograph of 1937
  was protected on 1 January 1996, so United States copyright runs to the
  end of 2032. The archive marks `M433` and `M436` restricted for the same
  reason.

Both should be marked restricted and `detail_only` until their terms end.

## 3. One public-domain record, and two marked permission granted, are still protected in the United States

- **`M111` *Paris qui brille*, 1931, by Zig.** Zig died in 1936. The poster
  was protected in France on 1 January 1996, so the restored United States
  term runs to the end of 2026. That is seven weeks away; until then the
  record should match the four 1931 Film-Kurier covers marked restricted
  (`M167`, `M175`, `M184`, `M189`).
- **`M104` and `M331`, *Der Kinematograph* advertisements of 1932.** Both
  are marked `permission_granted` and gallery-eligible. The permission is
  the Media History Digital Library's general statement on teaching and
  research use. That statement concerns the library's scans; the library
  does not hold the copyright. By the reasoning the archive applies to the
  1930 *Kinematograph* advertisements (`M037`, `M188`), an anonymous German
  advertisement of 1932 is protected in the United States to the end of
  2027.

## 4. Grounds that conflict with the archive's practice elsewhere

These need a decision, not necessarily a change of status.

- **Studio Harcourt.** `M249` Max Ophüls is marked public domain on the
  strength of a release recorded on Commons, whose VRT ticket cannot be
  checked. `M292` Clouzot and `M359` Joséphine Baker, also Harcourt, are
  marked restricted. The Baker record does so even though Commons holds
  permission documentation for it. Either the release holds for all three,
  or for none.
- **German photographs of 1932–33.** `M068` (Titzenthaler, Potsdamer Platz)
  rests on the photographer's death in 1937, which settles the term in the
  EU but not in the United States. `M248` (Alfred Zeisler, 1932 autograph
  card) rests on a Commons uploader's claim to own the print, and owning a
  print is not owning the copyright. Comparable German studio photographs
  of the 1930s, `M323` and `M344`, are marked restricted.
- **`M442` Emanuel Schlechter.** Commons tags the photograph PD-old and
  dates it to the “early 1900s”. Schlechter was born in 1904 and is an
  adult in the photograph, and no author is named, so PD-old cannot apply.
  The Polish rule for unreserved photographs probably does apply. The note
  should rest on that rule, or the record should be marked undetermined.
- **`M220` Ernst Lubitsch.** Commons dates the photograph 1949, two years
  after Lubitsch's death, and names only the Brazilian Arquivo Nacional as
  its source.
- **Twelve notes give no ground at all.** They read “The linked source
  identifies this item as public domain or otherwise free of known
  copyright restrictions”: `M043`, `M079`, `M080`, `M109`, `M136`, `M152`,
  `M156`, `M160`, `M168`, `M196`, `M228` and `M232`. Most are sound in
  substance, being Polish archive photographs or MGM posters without
  notice. `M043` Fritz Rotter (Becker & Maass, undated) and `M156` *Trouble
  for Two* have no stated basis. `M170` and `M196` rely on PICRYL, which
  has no standing on rights.

## 5. Kaper's own sheet music carries a public-domain badge

`M125`, `M126`, `M199`, `M200` and `M206` show editions of 1926–27
published under the name Asper. They are public domain in the United States
because the restored terms ended in 2021–22. Their notes say correctly that
the music is protected in Poland and the EU until 2053. The notes discuss
the music and the words but not the cover artwork, which has its own
author. The site nevertheless shows a plain **Public domain** badge, and
one file (`jak-sie-da.png`) is the first page of the music itself.

**Decision needed:** whether *public domain* on this site means the United
States only, which would need saying on the media page, or the United
States and Poland, which would make these five `mixed_rights`.

## 6. Restricted images are not confined to their records

`detail_only` keeps a file out of the galleries and nothing more. Restricted
and undetermined images also appear in other places:

- the **home page**: `M046` Kaper with Jurmann, and `M074` the *You're All
  I Need* cover;
- **life.html**: `M038`, `M046`, `M074`, `M121`, `M231`;
- the **people index**, as avatars: 13 portraits;
- as **hero images** of 16 Timeline Events;
- across person, organization, source and place pages.

Thirteen notes say the image is “confined to the film record” or “to the
person record”. For six of them this is not true: `M038`, `M046`, `M069`,
`M077`, `M164` and `M167` are also shown elsewhere.

**Decision needed:** either the renderers should skip restricted media in
hero, home, timeline and avatar slots, or the notes should stop saying
“confined”.

## 7. Thirty-seven restricted files are served at full size

The portrait notes often record a reduced resolution, and the reduction
matters to a fair-use or quotation argument. Covers, posters and clippings
do not record one. Thirty-seven restricted or undetermined files are larger
than 1000 px on the long side. Some examples:

- `M181` *Die Zwei vom Südexpreß* cover, 2222 × 2917;
- `M155`, 1952 × 2560;
- `M116`, 1859 × 2560;
- `M138`, `M133` and `M159`, all over 2200.

**Proposal:** cap these files at 800 px on the long side, and have the
validator enforce the cap.

## Checked and found sound

- **The validator's rule holds for every record:** each locally hosted
  restricted, undetermined or mixed-rights record is `detail_only`.
- **Every record has a rights note and a credit line.**
- **URAA arithmetic** is right in every restricted note. In the
  public-domain notes it is right except `M461` and `M111`.
- **United States government records** are correctly public domain: the
  arrival certificate, declarations, manifest, and the two portraits cut
  from them.
- **Registers and directories:** the Berlin marriage index, the address
  book and the baptism entry are correctly treated as records, not works.
- **Polish press and archive photographs** rest on the unreserved-photograph
  rule or on the Narodowe Archiwum Cyfrowe statement. Austrian simple
  photographs rest on the fifty-year term. Crown copyright and the Israeli
  and Italian photograph terms are applied correctly.
- **Open licences:**
  - CC BY, BY-SA and CC0 records carry their attribution;
  - the two CC BY-NC images, `M087` and `M092`, suit a non-commercial site;
  - `M281` (BY-NC-ND) is reproduced unaltered, apart from technical resizes.
- **External audio and video (96)** are linked, not rehosted.

## Applied, 12 November 2026

- **Renderings `M010`–`M014`.** The notes now name the legal basis for the
  music, which is the right of quotation (Polish Copyright Act, art. 29(1)).
  The renderings illustrate the archive's scholarly documentation and
  analysis of Kaper's early works, and they name the composer and the
  manuscript. The recording layer remains Iwona Lindstedt's own work.
- **Findings 2 and 3.** `M461`, `M319`, `M111`, `M104` and `M331` are now
  restricted and `detail_only`. Each note gives the term and the year it
  ends. `M111` becomes public domain in the United States on 1 January 2027.
- **Studio Harcourt.** The VRT ticket cited for `M249` (2020112910005534)
  is also cited on the Baker and Clouzot files, but there it supports a
  different claim: the term of a French collective work, not a release. All
  three Harcourt portraits are therefore treated alike, and `M249` is now
  restricted.
- **German photographs.**
  - `M043` (Becker & Maass / Marie Boehm) and `M068` (Titzenthaler) are now
    copyright undetermined, following `M350`. Both are public domain in the
    EU; in the United States the status turns on a first publication that
    is not attested.
  - `M248` (Zeisler) is now restricted, following `M344`.
- **Commons tags without provenance.** `M442` Schlechter, `M322` Laemmle and
  `M220` Lubitsch (whose Arquivo Nacional licence template could not be read
  here; Commons was rate-limiting)
  are now copyright undetermined, following `M309` and `M314`.
- **The twelve generic notes** now state their grounds:
  - the four Polish archive photographs rest on the unreserved-photograph
    rule and the Narodowe Archiwum Cyfrowe statement;
  - `M079` is an anonymous postcard of 1908;
  - the four Commons MGM posters, `M156` among them, rest on publication
    without notice;
  - the two PICRYL posters say that PICRYL is not a rights authority;
  - `M228` *Motion Picture Daily* is absent from the University of
    Pennsylvania renewal inventory.

- **Confined.** The six notes that said “confined” now say “excluded from
  the galleries”, which is what `detail_only` does.

**Left for the next pass:** findings 5 to 8. They are the public-domain
badge on Kaper's sheet music, restricted images in featured places, file
sizes, and the yearly review. A further point belongs with finding 6: the
four `mixed_rights` galleries (`M030`–`M033`) show restricted members such
as `M071`, `M105`, `M113` and `M121` in a gallery that is itself selected.
