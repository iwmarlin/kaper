# Title variants audit

21 November 2026. All 72 title variants were read against their Sources,
against the Works they belong to and against the archive's rule that only
what a document of the time confirms is entered as confirmed. Where a variant
rested on a secondary Source alone, the catalogue of the Bibliothèque
nationale de France (its SRU service, 293 records under Kaper's name) and the
trade papers in the Media History Digital Library were searched for the title.

## What a language-version variant is for

Forty variants repeat the title of a Work of their own: *Pourquoi donc verser
des larmes* is a variant of `W-S033` and also the title of `W-S036`, which a
`language_version_of` relation links to it. This looks like duplication and
is not. The record page uses such a variant in two places. On the original's
row for the related version it supplies the language badge and the printed
form of the title, and it is left out of the list of other titles so that it
is not shown twice. In a Source's citation it marks the title string with its
language for screen readers. Removing these variants would take both away, so
they stay. The rule of 7 November 2026, that a separate film's title is not a
variant of the film it was made from, concerns the catalogue search, where
such a variant makes one title find two films. See the open question below.

## What was changed

- **Two variants recorded twice.** *Keep the magic of Maytime* was entered
  twice on `W-S168` from the same 1936 copyright entry. `TV0280` was removed
  and `TV0315` kept. *Kinderlied* was entered twice on `W-S062`, once from the
  Paris edition, the UCLA folio and the Jurmann catalogue (`TV0313`) and once
  from Sieghardt and Görner (`TV0324`). They are now one variant, `TV0324`,
  with all five Sources, and its printed form is the one the 1932 Paris
  edition gives, “Kinderlied”, not Sieghardt's capitals. The song records
  `S062` and `S168` point at the surviving variants.
- **“Stand Condemned” was missing its first word.** The United States title
  of *Moscow Nights* (`W-F032`) is *I Stand Condemned*. The Film Daily Year
  Book 1937 (new `SRC1025`, p. 183) lists it among the 1936 releases:
  distributed by United Artists, made in England, with Harry Baur, Penelope
  Dudley Ward and Laurence Olivier, produced by Alexis Granowsky, directed by
  Anthony Asquith, reviewed 9 June 1936.
- **“Laubenkolonie” confirmed in the trade press of the time.** The variant
  of `W-F001` *Die lustigen Musikanten* rested on filmportal.de alone.
  Der Kinematograph of 25 August 1930 (no. 197, new `SRC1026`) reports that
  Dr. B. Kaper was engaged for the music of the Aafa sound film
  *Laubenkolonie*, with Fritz Rotter and Robert Gilbert writing the words of
  his songs. The issue of 3 November 1930 (new `SRC1027`) reports that
  *Laubenkolonie* (*Die lustigen Musikanten*) was held over for a second week
  at the Primus-Palast. The first is also now a Source of Kaper's composer
  credit on the film, which until now cited filmportal.de alone.
- **Period editions the archive already held.** Four variants cited only
  Sieghardt or a later catalogue, while the edition of the time was already a
  Source of the version's own Work. Each now cites it first:
  `TV0110` *Pourquoi donc verser des larmes* (Eschig, 1932, `SRC0981`),
  `TV0111` *Stay ’way from my arms* (copyright entry 1932, `SRC0212`),
  `TV0124` *Dit’s-moi, Madame* (Eschig, 1932, `SRC0950`) and
  `TV0151` *Ninon, quand tu me souris* (1933, `SRC0298`).
- **Working notes left in public notes.** Five variants from the BnF
  (`TV0298` to `TV0302`) ended with “Created 2026-07-05 … SRC record to be
  added”, although each has had its Source for months. The sentence was
  removed.

## Checked and left as they are

- **Film titles from filmportal.de** with no confirmation found in the trade
  papers: *Führerschein ins Glück* (`TV0013`), *Der Jazzbandit* and *Der Held
  des Tages* (`TV0032`, `TV0033`) and *Amor an der Leine* (`TV0039`). Variety
  of September 1933 reports an Ufa film *Amor an der Leine* banned in
  Teplitz-Schönau for want of Czech subtitles. That fits *Kind, ich freu’ mich
  auf Dein Kommen*, an Ufa film, but the item does not name it, so it was not
  added. filmportal.de draws on the censorship records and stays as the
  Source.
- **Variants that rest on Sieghardt alone** and for which the BnF catalogue
  holds nothing: *Blaue venezianische Wasser*, *Sous le ciel de Venise*,
  *San Francisco, sur notre terre immense il est un coin*, *Tous les noirs*
  and *Demain matin tout ira mieux* (`TV0289`–`TV0294`). The version Works
  they name rest on Sieghardt too. Their place in the archive is a question
  for those Works, not for the variants.
- *J’ai un message pour toi* (`TV0252`): La Dépêche of 6 April 1938 (`SRC0934`)
  reviews Joséphine Baker's record of it but names no songwriter, so it cannot
  confirm that this is the French text of Kaper's song.
- The forms kept on purpose by the works audit of 7 November 2026: *Così,
  cosa*, *Swing, Mister Mendelssohn*, *Ich geh’ nie von dir fort*, *O Madonna*,
  *Never Was There Such a Perfect Day*, *On Blue Venetian Waters* and
  *Sous le ciel de Venice*.
- The date of the Odeon disc of the Polish versions (`SRC0633`) is correctly
  recorded as after 1930, which is all the Biblioteka Narodowa states.

## Open question

Four films still carry the title of another film as a variant: *Mariage à
responsabilité limitée* (`TV0012`) on *Ehe mit beschränkter Haftung*, *Voyage
de noces* (`TV0019`) on *Hochzeitsreise zu Dritt*, *Moscow Nights* (`TV0050`)
on *Les nuits moscovites* and *Son altesse l’amour* (`TV0286`) on *Ihre
Majestät die Liebe*. Each of these films has its own record. Under the rule of
7 November these variants should go, as four others did then. However, the
public data audit records that `F005` and `F032` were made to carry two of
them deliberately. Left for a decision.
