Using the archive

Cite and reuse

How to cite the archive and its records, how to download the data behind them, and what the rights and certainty marks on each record mean.

The archive is a guide to documents held elsewhere. Cite those documents for the facts, and the archive for what it adds to them.

Cite the archive

Bronisław Kaper: A Source-Based Archive, 1902–1939. Bronisław Kaper Research Archive. https://iwmarlin.github.io/kaper/. Accessed [date].

If you work from the data rather than the pages, add the data version: the date given as publicDataUpdatedAt in the manifest.

Cite a record

Every record has a permanent identifier, shown above its title — P009 for a person, W-S229 for a work, SRC0742 for a source — and a permanent address built from it.

“O chant d’amour de Tahiti !” (W-S229). In Bronisław Kaper: A Source-Based Archive, 1902–1939. https://iwmarlin.github.io/kaper/records/work/W-S229/. Accessed [date].

Identifiers are never reused. When two records are merged, the retired address leads to the one that remains. A printed record page ends with its own address.

Cite the evidence, not only the archive

For a fact — a date, a credit, a registration — cite the document that states it. Each source record gives a full citation and, where one exists, a link to the holding institution’s copy. Cite the archive for what it adds: an identification, a comparison of sources, a note that weighs them.

Download the data

Everything the pages show comes from fourteen JSON tables in data/public/v1/. The manifest lists every file with its size and SHA-256 checksum, the date the data last changed, and the rules of the export.

Works
works.json, with the details of each kind in songs.json, films.json and other-works.json; relations between works, such as language versions and songs used in films, in work-relations.json; alternative titles in title-variants.json
People and organizations
people.json, person-name-variants.json, organizations.json
Credits
contributions.json — each credit joins a work to a person or an organization, in a role, and names the sources behind it
Evidence
sources.json and media.json
Time and place
timeline-events.json and places.json

Records refer to one another by identifier, and every link is kept in both directions.

Licence. No licence for reusing the data or the editorial text has been published yet. Quotation with citation is welcome; for anything beyond that, please ask.

Rights on images and sound

Each media record states its rights, with a note on how they were assessed. The statement describes the archive’s own use of the item; it does not give you permission to reuse it. For that, ask the rights holder named in the credit.

Public domain
Out of copyright, as the rights note explains.
Open licence
Published under a licence that permits reuse, named in the credit.
Reuse under provider terms
The holding institution allows reuse on its own terms, linked from the record.
Permission granted
Reproduced here by permission of the rights holder; that permission does not extend to you.
Own work
Made for the archive.
Copyright undetermined
The status could not be established. A reduced copy is shown for identification only.
Restricted use
In copyright or not cleared. A reduced copy is shown for scholarly identification only.
External content · not hosted
The archive holds no copy and links to the item where it is published.
Mixed rights
The parts of a gallery differ, and each part says which applies.

Certainty and reliability

Confirmed
A source states the credit, date or relation.
Probable
The evidence points to it but does not settle it, and the note says why.
High, medium, low
The weight given to a source. High for documents of the time, official registers, national-library and archival catalogues and established reference databases; medium for later compilations, general reference works and copies whose description cannot be checked against the object; low where the details rest on an uploader’s word alone.

A work is dated by the earliest year it is documented: a dated manuscript, a copyright registration or register entry, a publication, a recording session, or the release of the film it was used in. A film is dated by its first release.

Report a correction

If you find an error, or know a source the archive has missed, please open an issue in the archive’s repository. Name the record by its identifier and the source that shows the correction. A GitHub account is needed.
