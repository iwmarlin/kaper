Citation

“It pays to be wacky, so — ‘The fly outflew the flea’.” Chicago dispatch, with a photograph captioned “‘The lyric is lousy’ — so said Terry Shand, left. But neither Larry Shay, center, who helped Shand write the music, nor Jimmie Eaton, right, who wrote the words to ‘The fly outflew the flea’ care so long as the public likes the song.” Santa Ana Journal (Santa Ana, Calif.), vol. 3, no. 60, 9 July 1937, p. 18. California Digital Newspaper Collection, University of California, Riverside. Accessed 26 August 2026.

Evidence recorded in this source

The caption places Shay in the middle of the three men and states his part in the song: he helped Terry Shand write the music to a lyric by Jimmie Eaton. The interview quotes him on how the piece came about — “Jimmie wrote the lyric and Terry and I fitted in the music. It has catchy tempo and words and that is all you need” — and an unnamed publisher sets out what launching a song then cost: three or four thousand dollars for printing, artwork and orchestration, and sixty to two hundred and fifty dollars a week for the men who placed it with radio singers. The dispatch is datelined Chicago and was carried by a wire service, so the same text and picture appeared in other papers.
