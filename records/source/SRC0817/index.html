Identity assessment

Two lyricists of the same name were at work in Paris in the 1930s: the chansonnier Léo Lelièvre (1872–1956), born in Reims, and his son Léo Edmond Lelièvre (1899–1966), a member of SACEM from November 1919, who wrote for the Casino de Paris beside Henri Varna and whose covers name him “Léo Lelièvre fils”, among them “Gosse de Paris” (1929) and “Le Tambour-major” from the revue “Paris qui brille” (1931). Both men worked on “Parade de France” in 1934: the Salabert prints of Maïotte Almaby’s créole numbers “La Vraie Créola” and “Ma Goulou Goulou” read “paroles françaises de Léo Lelièvre père”, while the Coda prints of the Jurmann–Kaper songs carry the undifferentiated form. The BnF catalogue is divided over those Coda prints: the notices for the illustrated issues and for the chant-et-piano prints of “C’est chez nous” and “Un baiser de femme sous un ciel de feu” link the son, whereas notices for three further printings of “Un baiser de femme sous un ciel de feu”, including Paul Saegel’s orchestral arrangement, still link the father. The son is preferred on three grounds: the printed “père” used for the other Lelièvre inside the same revue, the continuity of the Varna partnership at the Casino de Paris from 1929 onwards, and the weight of the revised catalogue notices.
