About this place

Paramount’s French studio stood on the rue des Réservoirs in Saint-Maurice. The trade called it Joinville, after the neighbouring commune, where the Pathé-Natan studios stood on the boulevard Gallieni; Ciné-Miroir set out the difference in 1934. By May 1930 it had five stages under Robert T. Kane and served Paramount’s plan to make talking pictures in six languages; by 1933 it also housed independent productions. In September 1933 Variety placed Erich Pommer’s Fox-Europa unit there as it prepared its next film, which became On a volé un homme, with music by Jurmann and Kaper; in January 1934 it expected the unit to return to “the Paramount Joinville studio”. The marker stands on the rue des Réservoirs and does not outline the studio grounds.
