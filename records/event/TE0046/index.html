Koszutski Girls and the B. Asper pseudonym

Koszutski Girls complicates a simple division between Kaper’s concert training and his later popular-song career. Although preserved as an autograph manuscript, the piece is tied to Warsaw revue culture and to the international fashion for “girls” troupes. Its practical performance marking and the signature “Mus. B. Asper” foreshadow the pseudonymous popular-song activity of 1926–1927, making it a useful early marker of Kaper’s movement between concert idiom, cabaret practice, and commercial entertainment.
