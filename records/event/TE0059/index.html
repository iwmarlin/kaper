Kaper’s name in German film credits

Film CareerEuropean
Date
1933
Period
European · 1926–1934
Precision
Year
Place

Category
Film Career

Event

In April 1933 the Polish daily Nowiny Codzienne reported from Berlin that German censorship had removed the name of “p. Bronisław Kaper,” a Pole from Warsaw, from the German version of Ein Lied für Dich, which had just had its premiere at the Gloria-Palast; no other source confirms this. When Ufa released Kind, ich freu’ mich auf Dein Kommen in June, neither Kaper nor Jurmann was named in the credits or the advertising, which gave the music to Hans O. Borgmann. The pattern was not uniform, however. In January 1933 the Berlin censors had banned the poster for Madame wünscht keine Kinder, judging it, together with the film’s title, liable to overexcite young viewers; the decision did not concern the music. Shown as Madame wird kinderlieb, the film was advertised in Bielefeld on 25 August 1933 with the credit “Musik von Jurmann und Kaper,” on the same page as an advertisement for Hans F. K. Günther’s Kleine Rassenkunde des deutschen Volkes, and a Stuttgart cinema notice of September 1934 still named Jurmann and Kaper as its composers.
