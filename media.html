Song Samples & Early Works

European Period

Jak się da, to się zrobi

From "Perskie Oko" cabaret repertoire (1926) • Lyrics: Andrzej Włast • as Bruno Asper

One of Kaper's earliest commercial songs, published under his stage name during his Warsaw period. Source: Syrena-Grand-Record: № 17919 mx: 17919, 1926; Performers: Perskie Oko Theatre Orchestra conducted by Zygmunt Wiehler

European Period

Miss Anabelle Lee

Piano duo arrangement

This is a popular American song from 1927, written by Sidney Clare, Lew Pollack, and Harry Richman. The piece is an example of 'Tin Pan Alley,' representing the popular music of the Roaring Twenties. Source: Polydor 21768, 1928; Performers: Hermann Bick and Dr. Kaper.

European Period

Süße Mamsell, denkst du noch an das kleine Hotel

Tango (1929) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter • With Fritz Rotter

Early collaboration with Fritz Rotter, showcasing Kaper's European tango style before his film career. Source: Grammophon. Eine Schellackplatte aus dem Schallarchiv des Radiomuseum Hardthausen; Performers: Ilja Livschakoff Tanz-Orchester

European Period

Blaue Jungs von der Marine

From "Der Korvettenkapitän" (1930) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter

One of Kaper's first compositions for German cinema, marking his transition from independent songwriter to film composer. Source: Kristall 78N. 1930; Performers: Kurt Mühlhardt & Bernard Etté und sein Orchester

European Period

Spiel' mir auf der Balalaika einen Russischen Tango

Tango (1931) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter • With Fritz Rotter

Popular tango demonstrating Kaper's ability to blend Eastern European musical elements with contemporary dance styles. Source: Electrola E.G.2392, 1931; Performers: Marek Weber und sein Orchester

European Period

O Madonna!

from "Ein Lied für dich" (1933) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter, Ernst Marischka • With Walter Jurmann

A representative example of Kaper's European period. Song from UFA production, showcasing the Kaper-Jurmann partnership's European sophistication. Performer: Jan Kiepura

Hollywood Period

Someone to Care for Me

From "Three Smart Girls" (1936) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • With Walter Jurmann

Song from Deanna Durbin's film debut at Universal Pictures. Demonstrates Kaper-Jurmann's work outside of MGM, showing their adaptability to different studios and performers during their Hollywood period. Source: Parlophone (A6766); Performers: Victor Silvester and His Ballroom Orchestra

Hollywood Period

All God's Chillun Got Rhythm

From "A Day at the Races" (1937) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • With Walter Jurmann

Composed for the Marx Brothers film, this song transcended its original context to become a jazz standard. It demonstrates Kaper's remarkable success in creating music that merged European sophistication with American swing, becoming part of the broader jazz repertoire. Source: Master (MA 137); Performers: Duke Ellington and His Orchestra

Film samples & Context

Film sample (European)

Wenn mich die Männer betrügen

From "Alraune" (1930) • Performer: Brigitte Helm — Educational Use

The song — a tango from the feature film 'Alraune'.

Film sample (European)

Heut' bin ich gut aufgelegt

From "Ein toller Einfall" (1932) • Performer: Willy Fritsch & Rosy Barsony — Educational Use

The song — a foxtrot from the feature film 'Ein toller Einfall'. This song became one of the popular entertainment pieces of the pre-war era, with its light melody and cheerful mood reflecting the lively spirit of the Roaring Twenties.

Film sample (European)

Ninon

From "Ein Lied für Dich" (1933) • Performer: Jan Kiepura — Educational Use

"Ninon (Ninon, Lach mir einmal zu)" from 'Ein Lied für Dich' - one of Kaper's successful songs for the German cinema, co-composed with Walter Jurmann and performed by Jan Kiepura.

Film sample (European)

Funiculì funiculà

From "Abenteuer am Lido" (1933) • Performer: Alfred Piccaver — Educational Use

Neapolitan song by Luigi Denza. Model for the song 'Così, Cosa'

Film sample (European)

Natacha

From "Les nuits moscovites" (1934) • Lyrics: André de Badet • With Walter Jurmann — Educational Use

Foxtrot russe song from Kaper's European period, composed for French cinema during his final years before emigration to Hollywood.

Film sample (Hollywood)

Così, Cosa

From "A Night at the Opera" (1935) • With Walter Jurmann • Lyrics: Ned Washington — Educational Use

One of Kaper and Jurmann's most memorable compositions for the Marx Brothers film. This excerpt showcases their distinctive melodic style that bridged European sophistication with American entertainment sensibilities.

Film sample (Hollywood)

Someone to care for me

From "Three Smart Girls" (1936) • With Walter Jurmann • Lyrics: Gus Kahn — Educational Use

One of Kaper and Jurmann's songs for American musical comedy film directed by Henry Koster, featuring Deana Durbin.

Film sample (Hollywood)

San Francisco

From "San Francisco" (1936) • With Walter Jurmann • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • MGM Production — Educational Use

The title song that became synonymous with the city itself. Written during Kaper's most productive Hollywood period, it exemplifies his successful adaptation to American musical sensibilities while maintaining his European compositional sophistication.

Film sample (Hollywood)

All God's Chillun Got Rhythm

From "A Day at the Races" (1937) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • With Walter Jurmann — Educational Use

A swing-influenced number that demonstrates Kaper's versatility and adaptation to contemporary American musical trends of the late 1930s.

Film sample (Hollywood)

Swing Mr. Mendelssohn

From "Everybody Sing" (1938) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • With Walter Jurmann — Educational Use

A swing arrangement of Mendelssohn's Spring Song demonstrating Kaper's versatility and adaptation to contemporary American musical trends of the late 1930s.
