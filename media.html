Song Samples & Early Works

European Period

Jak się da, to się zrobi

From "Perskie Oko" cabaret repertoire (1926) • Lyrics: Andrzej Włast • as Bruno Asper

One of Kaper's earliest commercial songs, published under his stage name during his Warsaw period.

European Period

Miss Anabelle Lee

Piano duo arrangement

This is a popular American song from 1927, written by Sidney Clare, Lew Pollack, and Harry Richman. The piece is an example of 'Tin Pan Alley,' representing the popular music of the Roaring Twenties. Kaper recorded this transcription together with Hermann Bick for two pianos for the Polydor label.

European Period

Süße Mamsell, denkst du noch an das kleine Hotel

Tango (1929) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter • With Fritz Rotter • Ilja Livschakoff Tanz-Orchester

Early collaboration with Fritz Rotter, showcasing Kaper's European tango style before his film career.

European Period

Blaue Jungs von der Marine

From "Der Korvettenkapitän" (1930) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter

One of Kaper's first compositions for German cinema, marking his transition from independent songwriter to film composer.

European Period

Spiel' mir auf der Balalaika einen Russischen Tango

Tango (1931) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter • Performer: Marek Weber und sein Orchester • With Fritz Rotter

Popular tango demonstrating Kaper's ability to blend Eastern European musical elements with contemporary dance styles.

European Period

O Madonna!

from "Ein Lied für dich" (1933) • Lyrics: Fritz Rotter, Ernst Marischka • Performer: Jan Kiepura • With Walter Jurmann

A representative example of Kaper's European period. Song from UFA production, showcasing the Kaper-Jurmann partnership's European sophistication.

Hollywood Period

Someone to Care for Me

From "Three Smart Girls" (1936) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • Performer: Victor Silvester and His Ballroom Orchestra • With Walter Jurmann

Song from Deanna Durbin's film debut at Universal Pictures. Demonstrates Kaper-Jurmann's work outside of MGM, showing their adaptability to different studios and performers during their Hollywood period.

Hollywood Period

All God's Chillun Got Rhythm

From "A Day at the Races" (1937) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • Duke Ellington and His Orchestra • With Walter Jurmann

Composed for the Marx Brothers film, this song transcended its original context to become a jazz standard. It demonstrates Kaper's remarkable success in creating music that merged European sophistication with American swing, becoming part of the broader jazz repertoire.

Early Works

Rozmowa (Conversation)

A Concert Song for voice and piano (1923) • Lyrics: unknown

Kaper's earliest extant composition was written in Warsaw and survives in manuscript. This digital MIDI performance recreates the original manuscript, offering insight into Kaper's early compositional style before his commercial career began.

Film samples & Context

Film sample (European)

Heut' bin ich gut aufgelegt

From "Ein toller Einfall" (1932) • Performer: Willy Fritsch & Rosy Barsony

The song – a foxtrot from the feature film 'Ein toller Einfall'. This song became one of the popular entertainment pieces of the pre-war era, with its light melody and cheerful mood reflecting the lively spirit of the Roaring Twenties.

Film sample (European)

Funiculì funiculà

From "Abenteuer am Lido" (1933) • Performer: Alfred Piccaver

Neapolitan song by Luigi Denza. Model for the song 'Così, Cosa'

Film sample (European)

Natacha

From "Les nuits moscovites" (1934) • Lyrics: André de Badet • With Walter Jurmann

Foxtrot russe song from Kaper's European period, composed for French cinema during his final years before emigration to Hollywood.

Film sample (Hollywood)

Così, Cosa

From "A Night at the Opera" (1935) • With Walter Jurmann • Lyrics: Ned Washington – Educational Use

One of Kaper and Jurmann's most memorable compositions for the Marx Brothers film. This excerpt showcases their distinctive melodic style that bridged European sophistication with American entertainment sensibilities.

Film sample (Hollywood)

San Francisco

From "San Francisco" (1936) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • MGM Production – Educational Use

The title song that became synonymous with the city itself. Written during Kaper's most productive Hollywood period, it exemplifies his successful adaptation to American musical sensibilities while maintaining his European compositional sophistication.

Film sample (Hollywood)

All God's Chillun Got Rhythm

From "A Day at the Races" (1937) • Lyrics: Gus Kahn • With Walter Jurmann – Educational Use

A swing-influenced number that demonstrates Kaper's versatility and adaptation to contemporary American musical trends of the late 1930s.
